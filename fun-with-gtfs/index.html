Fun With GTFS

For about six months back in 2022, GTFS was my obsession. The General Transit Feed Specification (or GTFS) is one of the many unsung heroes of city life in the 21st Century. GTFS is a common data standard used by public transportation systems across the globe. It means that if a software application wants to work with transit data, it simply needs to decode the standard file structure outlined by GTFS. 

For someone interested in transportation, GTFS provides a treasure trove of opportunities. If I want to know where each bus stop is in Nairobi, Kenya --- it's easily available. If I want to explore the timetables for the metro system of Fortaleza, Brazil --- they've got that too.

When I worked for the regional planning agency serving the Louisville metropolitan area, I was approached by one of the planners for assistance in conducting a study of the efficacy of using Louisville's public transportation to commute to work. It was an interesting prospect and was similar to a study he had organized in New Orleans, which in turn was based off a study done by the University of Minnesota. 

GTFS turned out to be a key component in performing the spatial / temporal analysis required to conduct our study. I ended up communicating  with a developer at ESRI (not the last time I'd be doing that) where I helped identify a bug with their GTFS analysis toolbox, which was then resolved. 

Rather than explain the entire process in this blog post, I invite you to take a look at the Story Map I created to present our findings. I used this as a visual aid when I spoke about this project at both the 2022 Kentucky Association of Mapping Professionals conference and 2022 Indiana Association of MPOs conference. 

While I was crafting my presentation I stumbled upon an old write-up online detailing how to create neat visualizations of GTFS data, which I included in the StoryMap and is what you see at the top of this post. The write-up I found left a lot to be desired, so I had to make a lot of alterations. If you want to know how I did it, contact me!
