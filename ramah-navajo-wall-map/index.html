Ramah Navajo Wall Map

One of the perplexing things about moving up in the field of geography is that the higher you climb the career ladder, the fewer maps you are asked to make. While having more experience designing maps generally leads to the ability to create higher-quality products, when more responsibilities are given to a geographer, often the first category of task that is pushed down to lower-level employees is cartography.

This is why I was thrilled when the Ramah Navajo Department of Transportation specifically requested me to make a poster-sized map of their National Tribal Transportation Facility Inventory; which I had spent the last few months preparing for them. 

When producing this map, I looked to the seal of the Ramah Navajo for palette inspiration, which is what led to the choice of colors used in the boundaries of the reservation, legend box and routes themselves. 

I also generally shy away from using pure black text whenever possible. I find that it is too harsh on the eyes and I always try and opt for some other dark variant. In this case, I took a representative color sample from the aerial imagery and turned down the HSV values until I had a rich dark brown that was clearly legible but still felt at home in the desert landscape captured in the imagery. 

The client loved the map and told me they must guard it from others in their office who want to take it for themselves. I told her I'm always happy to make more.
