Site Selection Retrospective

Back when I was a student at the University of Louisville in the Geography department, one of the most interesting projects I was assigned was to perform a site suitability study.

A site suitability study is a straightforward concept, but in practice it can get complicated quickly. The idea is that given a certain set of criteria, geospatial analysis can find physical locations that best fit the conditions. There are two keys to good site selection.

The first is having a well-defined set of criteria. At a minimum these criteria should be categorized into desired traits vs required traits; ideally all conditions will be given a "weight" where some conditions are more important than others. 

The second key can be much harder to obtain (at least on the zero-dollar budget of a student!) and that is having quality supporting data. For a purely geographic study (e.g. finding the ideal location for a new campsite within a state park) the underlying data supporting the decision-making process is physical --- such as topography and hydrography --- and can usually be collected or obtained easily. In a commercial study, such as the case I used for my project, the kind of data needed is generally not the kind collected by public agencies --- such as locations of big box retailers and restaurants. Data that is collected by public agencies --- such as socio-economic factors and average daily traffic --- may only provided at a generalized level and may not always be the most representative. 

That being said, in my opinion having the well-defined criteria is the more important of the two keys. This opinion is backed up by my student project.

For my project I chose to search for the most suitable site for a new car wash in Louisville, specifically a Mike's Carwash. I spent my college years working at Mike's store number 45 and through that experience, I learned that the company had a very well-documented set of criteria they used when selecting a new site for an operation. 

I'll spare you the details of the entire process I used to calculate my final suitable site results, but essentially it involved dividing the city of Louisville into a grid of rectangles to serve as building blocks for suitable areas then calculating weighted values within each grid cell while taking into account the weighted values of nearby cells. All underlying data was obtained through publicly available means (who needs placer.ai) or physically mapped out by me to the best of my ability. Given the scope of this project (which was intended to be a proof of concept more than anything) I've always been pleased with how it turned out.

This map shows all the sites I identified as suitable for a new Mike's Location. Note that all Louisville Mike's locations and all sites identified by me are east of Interstate 65. Any native of Kentuckiana should not find that surprising given the criteria listed by the company.

This map of minimum bounding geometry helps illustrate the overlap better. The red polygon represents the area encompassed by all Mike's locations, the blue polygon represents the area encompassed by my suitable sites.

In the end, six car washes are located either completely within a suitable site or within 300 feet of a suitable site. Three of these are Mike's stores.

When I originally presented my project back in 2019, the single parcel that I determined to be most promising was home to a recently closed Payless shoe store. Everything about this site screamed potential for a car wash. If I wasn't a broke college student, I might have had a chance to make some money off my research because a few years later in 2022, someone saw the same opportunity as me and converted the site to an automatic car wash. Of course, I'm not privy to this company's financials, but based on the fact that I drive past this business every day as part of my commute to work --- it appears to be very successful.

It's nice to look back at something you did and know that you were on to something, even if it didn't result in any benefit to yourself. I'd really like to have an opportunity to do more of these kinds of studies in the future, not sure how that will play into a career in managing roadways for American Indian tribes... but who knows!
